RIVERSIDE COMMUNITY GARDEN - COMPOSTING GUIDE

Good compost needs a balance of "greens" and "browns". Aim for roughly one part greens
to two parts browns by volume.

Greens (nitrogen-rich): vegetable peelings, fruit scraps, coffee grounds, fresh grass
clippings, plant trimmings.

Browns (carbon-rich): dry leaves, shredded cardboard, straw, wood chips, egg boxes.

Never add: meat, fish, dairy, cooked food, diseased plants, weeds that have gone to seed,
or pet waste. These attract rats or spread disease.

Turn the heap every two to three weeks with a garden fork to add air. The heap should be
as damp as a wrung-out sponge; add water in dry weather and browns if it becomes slimy.

Compost is usually ready after 6 to 12 months. Finished compost is dark, crumbly and
smells earthy. The garden has three bays: add fresh material only to the left bay; the
middle bay is maturing; take finished compost only from the right bay.
